# Jones Junket — Act 3 Briefing: The Dying Light

---

## Story Brief

The final chamber is now a matter of history as much as danger.

Act 3 begins by comparing notes across all three houses. Build the temple's true timeline together. What did the Source give the QRians? What did it take from them? What did they choose at the end?

Jones's instinct is equally simple: destroying or burying the Source without understanding what it made possible would be a second tragedy. Push for a future in which truth survives, even if access must be constrained. If the room decides to close the book, make sure it does not also burn the archive.

---

## House Identity

Jones Junket are academic disciples on their maiden expedition, eager, idealistic, and meticulous. They prepared for years. Their instinct is scholarly and reverential — read the walls, decode the language, treat every inscription as a signpost. They carry pencils. They take notes. They believe in getting it right over getting it fast.

**Act 3 temptation:** Academic glory. "Save for Research" means a monumental discovery that launches their careers and honors Jones's legacy.

---

## How Act 3 Works

Act 3 has no missions in the traditional sense. Instead:

1. **Share your History Fragment cards** with the other houses. Compare all twelve fragments across Drake, Jones, and Croft.
2. **Reconstruct the timeline together.** Arrange the twelve History Fragments in chronological order (they are numbered 1–12 but the numbers are hidden — you must determine the order from context). The host will verify the sequence when you present it.
3. **Deliberate on the Major Decision.** Each house holds one Major Decision card — read all three as a room. Negotiate which outcome the group will pursue.
4. **Negotiate Settlement Clauses.** Each house holds clause cards. Up to 5 clauses can be added to the final settlement. Read them, trade them, debate which to include.
5. **The host records the final outcome and clauses.** Each house will learn whether they feel Vindicated, Compromised, or Horrified by the settlement.

DO NOT GO LOWER. THE AIR BELOW MAKES THE MIND WRONG.

Do not pray there. Do not study there. Do not sleep near the bright stone. If you have been below and now wish only to count, sort, or return, you are already touched. Tell another and let them bind your hands.

### Fragment: Testimony of Sefa Before the Closing

*Recorded on the last day before the lower seal was set*

We had the land-killing compounds. We tested them on the moss terraces, and nothing green returned. We could have made the valley unlivable and called that virtue.

Instead we chose the temple, though it would take our own with it. I do not know if this was courage, vanity, or merely the last form our love of order could take.

I know only that we chose to leave the future a danger behind stone, not a world already ruined by our fear.

## Glyph System

The text below explains the QRian writing system you will encounter. Glyph notation `{{{WORD}}}` means the word is written in QRian glyphs — the translation key is on your clue cards.

### QRian Central Glyph System

The Central Glyph System is the primary QRian writing system used across all puzzle types. Each glyph maps to an English letter (A–Z) or digit (1–9) — 35 characters total.

**Design Principles:** All glyphs are based on a square/rectangular frame — "cube-like," matching the lore of a language shaped by the Source. Variation comes from which sides are open/closed, internal lines (horizontal/vertical), and dot placement. All roughly the same size — they line up in rows like text. Drawable by hand quickly. Share a family resemblance but are individually distinct.

**Usage:** The glyph font maps English letters to QRian glyphs — when you see {{{WORD}}} notation, the glyph sequence spells out those English letters. Your clue cards will give you the specific glyph-to-letter mappings you need to decode inscriptions.

**Multiple Glyph Systems:** While this is the Central Glyph System (letter/number substitution), other glyph modes exist: Conceptual glyphs (Type C) represent concepts/words, not letters; Mathematical glyphs (Type H) use glyphs as variables and operations; Temporal glyphs (Type L) describe time. The Central Glyph System is the foundation.
