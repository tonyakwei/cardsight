# Jones Junket — Act 3 Phase 1 (Solo Analysis)

---

## 1. Per-History-Card Analysis

---

### Fragment: Note Tied to a Box of Learning Blocks

**Summary:** An educator or administrator found children using their new learning tools compulsively in the middle of the night — building, counting, whispering — without being told to and apparently without wanting to stop. The note ends with a directive to keep the blocks out of sleeping areas. The tone is worried but not yet alarmed; the author frames it as a behavioral observation, not an emergency. The new "square script" is explicitly noted as an improvement over the old hand — it was recently adopted, and someone thought it was better. The unsettling detail is not the learning itself but the compulsive late-night quality: children who did not choose to stop even when they could have.

**Phase:** Early dependency — blessing tipping toward compulsion. The QRians have embraced the Source-influenced system (the square script being easier, more memorable) and are beginning to notice that it does not let go as cleanly as expected. This is the moment before alarm, when the concern is still phrased as a management problem ("keep them out of sleeping rooms") rather than a crisis.

**Questions this raises:**
- What is the "old hand" that was replaced? Was the original script harder because it predated Source influence, and if so, was its difficulty a feature — a friction that prevented compulsive use?
- Who adopted the new square script? Was it a top-down decision or did it emerge organically? If a leader decided to adopt a more "efficient" system and that system was Source-adjacent, the adoption itself may have been the infection vector.
- How old are these children? If they are young enough that their first exposure to writing was already the square script, their relationship to the Source may be different from adults who transitioned from the old system.
- What happened to these two specific children later?

---

### Fragment: Order to Break the Fifth Drain

**Summary:** This is a formal administrative order commanding that Valve Five (the sabotaged valve from Act 1's pipe puzzle) be destroyed by chisel before dusk. The reason given is that people have learned the drainage paths and are using them to return to the lower chamber — the "school" basement identified in Act 1 — unseen. The people returning claim they are "only checking a line of figures" and claim they can stop whenever they want. The author sees through this. The final lines are the most striking: "We do not break this passage because it failed. We break it because we can no longer trust ourselves with a way back." This is a civilization that has identified its own compulsive behavior and is engineering against it — removing the possibility of return because willpower alone has failed.

**Phase:** Obsession/containment — mid-to-late crisis. This document comes after the behavior has already escalated (people returning clandestinely) and the community has decided willpower is not enough. The destruction of Valve Five is the earliest confirmed act of deliberate self-restriction.

**Questions this raises:**
- What was in the lower chamber that people kept returning to? The Act 1 missions described it as a school — but if the bright stone (referenced in the Plain Warning fragment) was in the lower chamber, the compulsion to return may be Source exposure at its source location, not just learned behavior.
- "A line of figures" — this is a specific kind of compulsion. People aren't returning to pray or sleep; they're returning to do mathematics. This is consistent with the cognitive-enhancement model: the Source sharpens certain faculties (pattern recognition, counting, calculation) and people are drawn back to exercise them.
- Who gave this order? An authority figure who had not themselves been exposed, or someone who recognized their own vulnerability?

---

### Fragment: Plain Warning for the Lower Stair

**Summary:** A blunt, all-caps public notice. The message is simple: the air below causes cognitive damage, do not go down, and if you have already been there and now feel compelled to count, sort, or return, you are already affected and should tell someone and allow yourself to be physically restrained. The language strips out ceremony — "the air below makes the mind wrong" is as direct as QRian public health writing apparently got. The symptom identification is specific: wanting to count, sort, or return are the three named warning signs.

**Phase:** Late crisis — this is a public health warning from a community that has understood the mechanism and is trying to stop further exposure. The fact that it needed to be written at all (and that it was posted "after the old warnings were judged a failure") means there were earlier, gentler warnings that did not work.

**Questions this raises:**
- What did the "old warnings" say? The implication is they were softer, more deferential, perhaps framed as religious caution rather than medical fact. The shift to blunt language suggests escalation.
- "Do not sleep near the bright stone" — the stone is the Source, or at least one form of it. It produces light. Is it luminescent? Bioluminescent? Radioactive? This is the most specific physical description of the Source in any Jones fragment.
- "Let them bind your hands" — physical restraint as treatment. This is late-stage: the community has moved past persuasion to physical intervention. How many people were already so affected that they needed to be restrained?
- The warning names three specific compulsive behaviors: count, sort, return. Counting and sorting are both organizational/analytical behaviors. The Source seems to enhance quantitative and organizational faculties specifically, not creativity or language. This is consistent with Vesh's botanical-to-physiological documentation (Vesh was a careful observer, documenting stepwise sequences) and with the wall of repetitions (writing the same inscription was a copying/sorting behavior, not a creative one).

### Fragment: Testimony of Sefa Before the Closing

**Summary:** This is a first-person statement from Sefa — the QRian whose riddles Jones found in Act 2 — delivered on the last day before the lower seal was set. It is an ethical reckoning, not a justification. Sefa describes two options considered: (1) use land-killing compounds to make the valley unlivable, destroying the Source by making its environment uninhabitable; (2) seal the temple, which would kill ("take our own with it") those still inside, but leave the world intact. They chose option 2. Sefa's self-examination is rigorous: she does not know if the choice was courage, vanity, or love of order. But she articulates the moral structure clearly — they chose to leave the danger behind stone rather than leave a ruined world. The Source is not destroyed; it is contained. And there are people inside the seal.

**Phase:** Sealing/end. This is the terminal document in the QRian self-imposed crisis arc. The sealing is confirmed as intentional, deliberate, and known to leave some people sealed inside. The land-killing compounds imply the QRians had the capability to destroy the Source outright but chose not to.

**Questions this raises:**
- "Take our own with it" — who was left inside? Were they already too far gone to be moved, or was the sealing itself the act of containment (some volunteered to stay, or were too exposed to leave safely)?
- What are the "land-killing compounds"? This is significant: the QRians developed weapons-grade materials and chose not to use them. That restraint is the moral crux of Sefa's testimony. But those compounds exist somewhere.
- "A danger behind stone, not a world already ruined by our fear" — this is the QRians' original version of the Recontain/Destroy debate. They chose Recontain, at the cost of lives inside. The current debate (Act 3) is a recapitulation of the same choice, with a third option (Open for Research) that the QRians did not seriously consider.
- Sefa's presence in both Act 2 (resistance riddles) and Act 3 (final testimony) makes her the most developed individual in the narrative. She is both an intellectual resister and a principled actor in the final crisis. Is she inside the seal or outside? The testimony is "before the closing," which does not resolve whether she set the seal from inside or outside.

## 2. Reference Card Review

---

### Major Decision Alignment

**Destroy the Source (Drake):**
Jones opposes this as primary position. Sefa's own testimony provides the direct counter-argument: the QRians had land-killing compounds (presumably more thorough than a modern Destroy approach) and chose not to use them specifically because destroying the container destroys context and leaves a ruined world. Jones's scholarly instinct is to read that choice as wisdom, not timidity. The Source in its current sealed state is also a record — destroying it is destroying evidence. Jones will not champion Destroy.

**Recontain the Source (Croft):**
Jones finds this acceptable as a fallback but argues it is incomplete. The QRians already tried Recontain; the current state of the temple (three rival expeditions all arriving simultaneously) is evidence that containment alone does not produce long-term stability. Recontain without documentation, publication, and archival access means the choice must be made again by future generations with less information than the present generation. Jones will accept Recontain if paired with strong archival clauses but will not champion it as the primary position.

**Open It for Research (Jones):**
Jones's primary position. "Truth must survive, even if access must be constrained." The Jones brief is explicit: "don't burn the archive." Controlled access is not the same as uncontrolled access. Jones will argue that the QRians' tragedy was not the Source itself but the absence of a protocol — they were exposed without warning, without restraint systems, without a method for protecting researchers while extracting knowledge. A modern controlled-access framework (with the No Judgment Without Record clause, archival copying, publication) is different in kind from what the QRians experienced. The Source can be studied safely if studied correctly.

**Jones's internal tension:** The Sefa testimony is a complication. Sefa's people had land-killing compounds and chose not to destroy, but they also did NOT open for research — they recontained. If Jones's hero in the narrative (Sefa, the clear-eyed resister) chose Recontain over Open, that is an argument Jones cannot dismiss. Jones must argue that the current situation is structurally different: the QRians sealed it without external witnesses, without a committed archival framework. The present room has all three houses, external attestation, and the ability to implement safeguards the QRians lacked.

---

### Clause Alignment

**Clauses Jones would champion:**

1. **No Judgment Without Record** — Jones's most important clause regardless of the major decision outcome. If the room votes Destroy, this clause is the difference between burning the library and saving it. If the room votes Recontain, this clause ensures the next generation has something to work from. If the room votes Open, this clause is the minimum viable safety protocol. This is non-negotiable for Jones in any scenario.

2. **Copy the Inscriptions** — Directly in Jones's scholarly lane. The wall inscriptions, the glyph system, the personal documents from Vesh and Sefa — this is an irreplaceable record of a civilization's encounter with a transformative substance. The knowledge of what happened here may be the most important thing the Source produced. Copying the inscriptions preserves the case study even if the Source is destroyed.

3. **Publish the Discovery** — Jones's identity is academic. Suppressing a discovery of this scale would be a betrayal of the scholarly mission. Jones will argue hard for this. Caveat: Jones can accept Withhold the Mechanism (held by Drake) as a paired clause that allows publication of the cultural/historical findings while redacting the dangerous technical details. Jones + Drake alignment on this pairing is possible.

4. **Preserve Some Artifacts** — Weaker priority than the above three but consistent. Physical artifacts (the learning blocks, Sefa's scroll-case, Vesh's compartment tiles, the stone vessels) are material evidence. Preservation is better than loss.

**Clauses Jones would accept without championing:**

5. **Three-House Custody (Croft)** — Jones would accept this. Shared custody prevents any single house from leveraging the discovery for private gain, which Jones doesn't want to do anyway. Paired with Publish the Discovery, Three-House Custody removes the conflict-of-interest concern.

6. **Three-Witness Rule (Croft)** — Acceptable. Jones would prefer it not block research access but could live with it as a procedural safeguard.

7. **Withhold the Mechanism (Drake)** — Jones accepts this as a companion to Publish the Discovery. The cultural record can be shared; the specific mechanism of Source transmission and compulsion can be redacted from public documents. Jones sees this as reasonable harm reduction.

**Clauses Jones would oppose:**

8. **Suppress the Location (Drake)** — Jones opposes this. Suppressing the location is strategically inconsistent with any of the major decisions: if the room Destroys, location is irrelevant anyway; if Recontained, future generations need to know where to watch; if Open for Research, suppression is explicitly contradictory. Additionally, the fact that three rival houses already know the location makes this clause unenforceable — the promise to suppress is only as strong as the least-committed house's discretion. Jones will argue this clause is both wrong in principle and ineffective in practice.

9. **No Ready Passage (Drake)** — Jones opposes if the major decision is Open for Research (no ready passage blocks the very thing Open supports). Jones would accept this only under Recontain.

10. **No Private Keeping (Drake)** — Jones has no private keeping ambitions (Jones wants to publish, not hoard) so this clause does not threaten Jones's interests. Jones would accept it but would not use a clause slot on it, since Jones's own behavior already satisfies it.

11. **No House Takes Credit (Croft)** — Jones is willing to accept this if it's a tie-breaker needed for consensus, but it slightly chafes against the academic identity (being credited for a discovery matters to scholars). Jones would trade away this concession for something it cares more about.

Comes after earlier softer warnings have already been posted and failed. The blunt language ("makes the mind wrong," "bind your hands") implies escalation from gentler early warnings. The community has identified that self-regulation does not work and is engineering against it. Terminal document. "Last day before the lower seal was set" is explicit. This is the endpoint. Confidence: highest. Sefa's testimony is the last known first-person QRian document in Jones's possession.

## 4. Jones's Opening Position for the Round Table

The Source did not corrupt the QRians — it amplified them, and they broke under the amplification. Every document in our possession describes people doing more of what they already did: children who loved learning studied past midnight, scholars who mapped drainage returned to map it again, writers who documented carved the same phrase until their chisels wore down. The Source did not change who they were. It removed their ability to stop.

What the QRians lacked was protocol. No restraint system for researchers. No archival framework that could preserve what was learned before the learning itself became compulsive. No external witnesses to hold the record. They chose containment because they had no other option that preserved the world outside. We have options they did not.

Jones's position is Open for Research, with conditions. The Source behind that stone is the only specimen we know of. Destroying it makes Sefa's choice permanent without the justification Sefa had — she was acting to save a world she could still ruin; we are not in that position, and no one is currently at risk. Containing it again without documentation means the next expedition in fifty years faces the same decision with less information than we have now.

Our condition is No Judgment Without Record — every artifact, inscription, and structural detail must be documented before any irreversible action is taken. If the room ultimately votes to seal or destroy, that clause ensures the record survives. If the room votes Open, it sets the floor for responsible access. It costs nothing and prevents the greatest possible loss: acting without knowing what we acted on.

Jones also proposes Copy the Inscriptions and Publish the Discovery, and will accept Withhold the Mechanism as a paired safeguard. The knowledge of what the Source does belongs to the world. The technical mechanism of how to weaponize it does not.

## 5. What Jones Needs from Other Houses

**Highest priority — needed to settle open questions:**

1. **What phase is the early blessing?** Jones holds no documents from before the compulsion was first noticed. The Act 3 briefing describes Act 3 as covering "what did the Source give the QRians" as a first question — but Jones's four fragments all describe the crisis phase or the end. Jones cannot reconstruct what the QRians got from the Source before it broke them. Other houses should hold earlier documents showing the Source in its beneficial phase — the initial cognitive gains, the explosion of scholarship, the period before anyone was worried. Without this, Jones cannot make the case that Open for Research is not simply repeating the original mistake.

2. **Is the bright stone physically locatable?** The Plain Warning references "the bright stone" as something that should not be slept near. The Source has a specific physical location and an observable property (luminescence). Do Drake or Croft hold fragments that describe where the stone is, what it looks like, or whether it has been moved?

3. **Who was sealed inside?** Sefa's testimony says the sealing "would take our own with it." Who were those people? Were they compelled individuals who could not leave, or were they people who chose to stay? The moral weight of the sealing changes significantly depending on the answer. Jones suspects other houses have documents that illuminate this — either records from people who were being sealed in, or records of the decision process about who could leave.

4. **What did the Wall of Repetitions say?** Jones held the Amber tile in Act 2 but never got the full inscription (required all three tiles pooled). The Wall's inscription is the only extended text the QRians produced under Source influence — knowing what it says would settle whether the compulsion was purely formal (copying the same thing) or thematically significant (what they kept trying to write reveals what the Source was communicating). This is a cross-house question about Act 2 outcomes.

5. **What is the mechanism of action?** Vesh's Compartment suggested airborne pollen/spore transmission. The Plain Warning says "the air below makes the mind wrong." Do Drake or Croft hold fragments that are more specific about what the Source is — biological, mineral, something else? The specificity matters for the Open for Research argument: "controlled access" means different things if the vector is a gas, a powder, or physical contact with a stone.

## 6. Final Story Log Entry

The temple was a school that became a quarantine that became a tomb, and the same people made all three of those choices. Jones arrived expecting to read the record of a civilization; instead Jones is reading the record of a civilization's encounter with something that reads back. The fragments from Act 3 pay off the Act 2 ambiguity directly: Vesh's botanical sequences were not metaphors, they were documentation — the Source propagates through the air and begins in the respiratory system — and the compulsive writing on the walls was not scholarship, it was the symptom. What Jones could not have predicted from Act 2 alone is the moral dimension of the sealing: Sefa and her contemporaries had land-killing alternatives and chose not to use them. That restraint is the most important fact in the archive. The QRians did not destroy the world; they destroyed themselves instead. Jones leaves Act 3 prep with no interest in finishing what fear would start.

## 7. Designer Flags

---

**Ambiguous fragment content:**

- **Note Tied to a Box of Learning Blocks:** The phrase "Keep them out of the sleeping rooms" reads as an instruction about the physical blocks, not the children. This is likely intentional (the author is managing the artifact, not the children) but a hasty reader may miss the horror: the author is not removing the children from the blocks, they are removing the blocks from the sleeping area. That framing (children are not the problem; access is) is the proto-logic of "break the drain." Consider a line that clarifies the author is worried about the children, not just the tool.

- **Order to Break the Fifth Drain:** "Night returns" in the header is the clearest phase indicator in any Jones fragment — it tells you compulsive behavior is already established. The phrase "they say this while already asking for the key" is excellent. No ambiguity problems here.

- **Plain Warning:** "The air below makes the mind wrong" is the most direct statement about the Source mechanism in Jones's entire three-act arc. It also retroactively confirms the Act 2 pollen hypothesis. This is the fragment that does the most explanatory work. Concern: if players encounter this fragment late in the round-table (because Jones doesn't lead with it), its explanatory value is diminished. Jones should read this aloud early.

- **Testimony of Sefa:** "I do not know if this was courage, vanity, or merely the last form our love of order could take" is excellent writing. Sefa's self-uncertainty about the sealing choice is what makes this testimony useful for deliberation — it refuses to sanctify the Recontain decision, which prevents any house from claiming Sefa as a pure moral authority for their preferred outcome. Flag for the designer: this is doing important rhetorical work. If this testimony were replaced with a more confident "we made the right choice," it would close off the deliberation before it begins. Protect this ambiguity.

**Reference card / clause concerns:**

- **No Judgment Without Record** — the wording says "chamber in its entirety must be fully documented." In a game context, "fully" is vague — does this mean document before the act ends, or before any out-of-game consequence is applied? If this is a clause that has mechanical weight (e.g., unlocks a certain outcome or prevents others), it needs a more specific threshold. As flavor, it works. As a procedural constraint, it needs a definition of "complete."

- **Withhold the Mechanism** — "dangerous technical details of how the Source works" is undefined. Players will ask: what counts as mechanism? The symptom description (the wall inscription, Vesh's documentation)? The location of the bright stone? The propagation pathway? This clause needs a one-sentence definition of what gets withheld, or players will argue endlessly about where the line is during deliberation.

- **Suppress the Location** — as noted in the clause review, this is logically inconsistent with the fact that three houses know the location. The clause needs to either acknowledge this (e.g., "all parties commit to not actively publishing the location") or be rewritten to address second-party disclosure rather than primary possession of the information.

**Act 1+2 carry-forward that did not pay off:**

- **The Pantry (Mission 1, Act 2)** — Jones flagged in Act 2 that the pantry preparations could be pharmacological countermeasures against the Source but the mission brief did not confirm this. Act 3 does not resolve this thread. The pantry remains unexplained in the larger narrative. If the QRians were developing counteragents, that matters for the current deliberation (were they on a path to safe coexistence before the temple was sealed?). If they were just eating, the mission is disconnected from the main arc. This is an unresolved thread.

- **Valve Five is broken in Act 1 (Mission 2)** — Jones now has the document that explains why (Order to Break the Fifth Drain). This is the best cross-act payoff in the entire playtest: the puzzle answer in Act 1 (the unlabeled sabotage element) is directly explained by a fragment in Act 3. This should be called out in the deliberation — Jones should surface the connection explicitly. Designer note: the payoff is there but it requires Jones to make the connection themselves. Consider whether the fragment should reference "the fifth valve" explicitly (it says "Valve Five" and "fifth drain" which is sufficient if players remember Act 1's pipe puzzle). Players who did not solve Act 1 Mission 2 personally may not have this context. The host should be prepared to surface it.

- **Sefa's scroll-case (Act 2, Mission 5)** — Jones flagged that the scroll-case behind Sefa's tile should have narrative content proportional to the setup. Act 3's Sefa testimony does pay off Sefa's character — but it doesn't answer what was in the scroll-case. If the scroll-case contained a draft of this testimony, or an earlier version of the warning, that would close the loop. As written, Sefa's testimony is a new document in Act 3, not a retrieval of what was in the scroll-case. Small gap; not critical.

**Pacing assessment for a 5-7 person Jones team:**

Jones holds four history fragments, four settlement clauses, and one major decision card. Reading time: approximately 8-10 minutes for all seven cards (the fragments are dense with implication and will prompt immediate discussion). Reaching positions on each clause: another 10-12 minutes (the Jones clauses are mostly aligned with Jones's identity, so internal disagreement will be low, but players will want to think about sequencing and trading). Drafting the opening statement: 5-8 minutes if a notetaker is designated; 10+ if the house debates the framing. Total for Phase 1 before the round-table: **25-30 minutes.**

Risk: if players spend significant time trying to reconstruct the timeline from Jones's four fragments alone (before learning that the full 12 fragments from all three houses are needed for the reconstruction), they will be frustrated and feel stuck. The story brief says "share your history fragment cards with the other houses" but this may not register until the team has already spent 10 minutes trying to sequence four cards. The host brief or the story sheet should make explicit upfront: "you cannot reconstruct the full timeline from your fragments alone — that is intentional."
