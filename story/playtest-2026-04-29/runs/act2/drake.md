The classic bronze alloy. Copper and tin smelted together = bronze. No ambiguity.

Flower essence + alcohol (spirit) = perfume. The distillation process for essential oils and aromatic spirits yields perfume. Also possibly TINCTURE (herbal extract in alcohol) or ESSENCE, but PERFUME is most natural for FLOWER+SPIRIT.

**Trade need:** Togom Riddle-Tablet II from Jones.

**Solving process:**

**Riddle I (Drake holds):** *"Round and round this bit, but it's not 'and.'"*

Parsing:
- "Round and round" → circular, cycling, rotating
- "This bit" → this piece / this small amount / this thing
- "But it's not 'and'" → it resembles "and" in function/type but is categorically different

Logical operator interpretation: "And" and "Or" are the two fundamental conjunctions (logical AND, logical OR). "Round and round this bit" — the OR gate creates a loop in logic (A OR B, B OR A, cycling). "But it's not 'and'" — definitively not AND. Therefore: **OR**.

Alternate reading: "Round" as a shape (circle = letter O), "round and round this bit" = O repeating = "OO"? Doesn't resolve to a clean word. Discard.

Another alternate: "Round" as a noun (a round of ammunition, a round of drinks), "this bit" = this round = a circular thing. A "bit" that goes round and round = a drill bit. A drill bit that's "not 'and'" = an "or" bit? Strained. Discard.

The cleanest reading: logical operators. "And" = AND. "Round and round, not 'and'" = OR (the cyclical alternative). Answer: **OR**.

**Riddle II (Jones holds):** Unknown. Cannot solve without the card.

**Final answer:** OR [WORD2] — cannot commit second word without Riddle-Tablet II.

- A clean Croft deal: Drake's Croft holdings (Krane Tile, Calculation Tablet, Lens-Label, Purple Tile, Yenus Riddle) cover Croft's needs, and Drake only needs 3 from Croft (Batch C, Slot 3, Red Tile 3, Bark Station 5 = actually 4). Still roughly balanced.

**Verdict:** The negotiation is feasible but Drake has a slight dependency on Jones goodwill for 1-2 extra cards. In a real room, this would likely close — but it would take 2-3 rounds of negotiation rather than a single clean swap. If Jones is also overloaded asking from Croft, the three-way trading dynamic could bottleneck. Recommend confirming that Jones's asks from Croft don't compete with Drake's asks from Croft for the same cards.

**One-sided risk:** If Jones is also a heavy requester from Croft, and Croft is simultaneously being asked by both other houses, Croft becomes a trading bottleneck. Verify Croft's position is not "everyone needs everything from Croft."

## End-of-act theory of the temple

The Green Department was a pharmaceutical and materials production facility — a working lab run by skilled practitioners. The five compounds in the Reagent Alcove (Bronze, Glass, Soap, Dye, Perfume) span metallurgy, chemistry, and cosmetics, suggesting the QRians had broad material science. The pharmacist's flower-grid is a sedative recipe. This reads as normal institutional activity for the first two missions.

But the Wall of Repetitions — an inscription carved hundreds of times to survive physical decay — is not normal documentation. That is compulsion or desperation. The mission blurb says they carved it "so the wall would still say it after the wall failed." They expected the temple to fail. They planned for their own erasure. That's fear, not scholarship.

Drevu sealed a personal compartment from his own colleagues using a word-progression passcode. Togom sealed a bunker using cryptic riddles. These are people who didn't trust each other. The institution fractured — something drove individuals to hide things from the collective. The carving of the east wall with a single repeated phrase may have been a response to that fracture: if no one can be trusted to remember, put it on the wall so many times that it survives even the forgetting.

The Source hasn't been explicitly named in Drake's Act 2 materials, but the Reagent Alcove framing — "They were arming against something" — is the clearest signal yet. The compounds read as weapons or weapon-adjacent materials. The sedative on the pharmacist's wall could be a delivery mechanism, not medicine. The QRians weren't just studying transformation (Act 1 theory) — they were preparing to fight something that was corrupting them or threatening the temple. The Green Department is an armory disguised as a laboratory.

Drake's end-of-act theory: the QRians discovered or were exposed to something they called a Source. Some of them were compromised by it (hence the institutional secrecy, Drevu's hidden compartment, the fractured trust). Others were preparing countermeasures (the armory compounds, Togom's sealed bunker). The repeated inscription is either a warning or a rallying cry — possibly the thing they needed to keep saying to themselves or each other because the Source was making them forget or doubt it. The institutional → environmental shift is complete: this is not a stable civilization. This is a civilization under external pressure, failing to hold together.

## Designer summary

### Hardest puzzles (ranked)

1. **Mission 1 (Powder of the Quiet Bed)** — hardest by logistics: grid-dependent mechanic that cannot function without the physical artifact, requires all three trades to complete, and has an ambiguous "stands alone" clause that needs clarification before the puzzle is even startable.
2. **Mission 5 (Reinforced Bunker, Riddle I)** — hardest by cognitive difficulty: cryptic wordplay requiring Boolean/logical-operator familiarity. Teams without that frame will stall significantly.
3. **Mission 3 (Wall of Repetitions)** — medium-high: 21-position majority-vote reconstruction across three traded cards is systematic but tedious; managing the three-tile comparison table under time pressure is the challenge.
4. **Mission 2 (Drevu's Compartment)** — medium: puzzle difficulty depends entirely on the progression type (known only once all tiles are pooled). Inferring a missing step in a word-progression is a solid, fair mechanic.
5. **Mission 4 (Reagent Alcove)** — easiest: pure chemistry knowledge recall, no mechanic to learn. Fast for any team with the relevant knowledge, stalled for teams without it.

### Puzzles with mechanic/clue/answer problems

- **Mission 1 — "stands alone" clause:** Un-numbered flowers are mentioned but their mechanical role is undefined. Does the flower contribute to the letter path or not? Clarify.
- **Mission 1 — grid dependency:** The puzzle cannot be evaluated without the printed artifact. This is correct design (physical-first), but it means the puzzle is invisible to analytical playtesting. Ensure the grid asset is production-ready before the live game.
- **Mission 4 — Station 4 (PLANT + STAIN):** Both DYE and INK are valid answers. Either tighten the reagent pairing or accept both in answer validation.
- **Mission 4 — Station 5 (FLOWER + SPIRIT):** PERFUME vs TINCTURE vs ESSENCE are all valid. Accept multiple answers or add a disambiguating detail.
- **Mission 4 — knowledge dependency:** No backup mechanic for teams without chemistry/alchemy knowledge. Add process-hint text to each bark label or accept a broader answer set.
- **Mission 5, Riddle I — "this bit" ambiguity:** "Bit" is doing too much (binary, small amount, drill bit). The Boolean reading is correct but non-obvious. The clue should be slightly more pointed toward the "choice/alternative" frame without giving away OR.
- **Mission 5, Riddle I — answer length:** OR is two letters. Ensure the answer validation for "OR [WORD2]" works cleanly and doesn't penalize short words.

### Puzzles that worked well

- **Mission 3 (Wall of Repetitions) — mechanic design:** Majority-vote corruption recovery across three tiles is genuinely elegant. Thematically resonant (the repetition is the preservation). The cross-house combine (three wall colors → three-line inscription) is the best structural puzzle design in Act 2.
- **Mission 4 (Reagent Alcove) — reagent pairs:** COPPER+TIN→BRONZE and SAND+HEAT→GLASS and FAT+ASH→SOAP are sharp, clean, and unambiguous. The first three stations are textbook.
- **Mission 2 (Drevu's Compartment) — mechanic concept:** Inferring the missing step in a word progression is a fair, self-contained puzzle type. The "compartment opens when you complete the sequence" conceit is satisfying.
- **Mission 5 (Reinforced Bunker) — narrative setup:** "Priest Physicist Togom" is an excellent character introduction. A scientist-priest who hid something from his own people and protected it with riddles is exactly the kind of individual character that makes the QRian civilization feel real and troubled rather than monolithic.

### Story coherence

The Source mythology does not land explicitly in Drake's Act 2 materials — the word "Source" appears only in the CLAUDE.md lore notes, not in any card or mission text I was given. However, the *environmental* shift still lands through the Reagent Alcove's "arming against something" framing and the Wall of Repetitions' compulsive repetition. By Mission 5, a thoughtful team will have built a theory of external threat without being handed the word "Source."

The institutional → environmental/threat shift happens at Mission 3 (Wall of Repetitions story log entry, above). Before that point: institutional. At and after: something is wrong. The transition trigger is clear: compulsive repetition = crisis behavior, not archival behavior.

**Gap:** Drake's Act 2 materials never name or even gesture toward what the threat is. "Arming against something" (Mission 4) and "sealed from his own people" (Mission 5) both imply a threat but identify nothing about its nature. Jones and Croft's materials presumably provide more (the lore document exists but was not read). If Drake completes all five missions without ever encountering the word "Source" or an explicit reference to external influence, the shift lands emotionally but not cognitively. Players will feel something is wrong without knowing what. That's dramatically fine for Act 2 (Act 3 presumably resolves it), but the "something" needs a shape — even a partial one — or Drake arrives in Act 3 with less narrative investment than the other houses.

**Recommendation:** Add one concrete reference to the Source (or the threat it represents) in either Mission 3's completion text (after reconstructing the wall inscription) or Mission 5's completion text (after breaching Togom's bunker). A single line: "Inside the scroll-case: a name, written and crossed out. The Source." Or similar. Give Drake something to carry into Act 3 besides a general sense of institutional collapse.

### Pacing

**Time pressure — real or fake?**

The host narration ("doors closing, maybe twenty minutes") is narratively vivid but mechanically toothless unless the host is actually timing the act. The missions vary significantly in setup time:
- Mission 4 (Reagent Alcove): can be started immediately with Drake's own bark labels for Stations 1-2, before trades are complete. Good anchor to start.
- Mission 3 (Wall of Repetitions): requires all three tiles but the mechanic is clear; start after first wave of trades.
- Mission 1 (Powder of the Quiet Bed): grid-dependent, needs trades AND the physical artifact. Cannot start until both trades close.
- Mission 2 (Drevu's Compartment): needs all three tile slots; start after second wave of trades.
- Mission 5 (Reinforced Bunker): needs Riddle-Tablet II from Jones; start mid-act once Jones deal closes.

**Where teams would stall:** Trading coordination is the primary bottleneck. If all three houses are simultaneously trying to negotiate, the first 10 minutes of the act may be pure chaos. The structure (Drake needs 9 cards, Jones needs N, Croft needs M) should be pre-analyzed by the host so they can identify and unblock any deadlock quickly.

**Where time pressure feels real:** The Wall of Repetitions (21-position reconstruction) and Mission 1 (grid tracing) both have enough mechanical overhead that a team can feel the clock. Mission 4 is fast. Mission 5 (if Riddle I is OR and Riddle II is accessible) is fast. Missions 2 is medium. Pacing is uneven — two fast missions, two slow, one grid-dependent unknown.

**Recommendation:** Give teams a clear suggested starting sequence at mission-brief reading: "Start Mission 4 immediately (you have your own bark labels for stations 1-2). Trade simultaneously for everything else. Attack Mission 3 next (it's systematic). Mission 1 after trades close (needs the grid). Mission 2 mid-act. Mission 5 last (it requires the fuse charges, which you've already committed to placing — it's your capstone)."

### Trading mechanic feedback

**Overall balance:** Drake's trading position is reasonably well-calibrated. We are a net receiver (9 cards in, 7 cards out) but hold high-value cards that other houses need. The negotiation should close with effort.

**Asymmetry concern:** Drake's asks from Jones (5 cards total: Batch B, Slot 2, Red Tile 2, Bark Labels 3+4, Riddle-Tablet II) vs. Drake's offers to Jones (Amber Wall Tile 2, Vesh Tile Slot 1 = 2 cards) creates a 5:2 imbalance. Even if Drake's 2 cards are high-priority for Jones, Jones has to give 5 to get 2. In a real room, Jones will likely require Drake to offer something else — possibly cards Drake is holding for Croft. This creates cross-house competition for Drake's card inventory.

**Croft bottleneck risk:** Drake needs 4 cards from Croft (Batch C, Slot 3, Red Tile 3, Bark Station 5). If Jones also needs significant Croft cards, Croft may be the most-solicited house in Act 2. Flag this for the host to monitor: if Croft is receiving too many simultaneous trade requests, they become a single-point bottleneck.

**Clean trades:** The wall-tile-for-wall-tile exchanges (Amber Tile 2 → Jones, Purple Tile 2 → Croft) are the cleanest, most symmetric deals in the act. Good design — those trades close themselves.

**Trading mechanic recommendation:** Add a brief physical trading phase protocol at the start of Act 2 (e.g., "first 5 minutes: declare your needs and offers"). Without a structured opening, trading devolves into simultaneous cross-table shouting. Even a 2-minute "needs board" (whiteboard or paper) where each house writes "WE NEED" and "WE HAVE" would dramatically improve trading efficiency.
